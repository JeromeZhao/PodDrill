# Orwell's rules for writing

Use this for the words in `summary` and for the words in each paragraph `text`. The rules are George Orwell's, from "Politics and the English Language", Horizon, April 1946. The wording below matches the [Orwell Foundation text](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/politics-and-the-english-language/).

Pick the word that matches the cue. Do not start from a ready phrase and bend the cue to fit it.

Apply all six. The last rule wins when another rule would make the sentence false, cruel, or unclear.

## Skip a worn figure

Never use a metaphor, simile or other figure of speech which you are used to seeing in print.

A figure you have seen in print carries no fact from this episode. Say what the cues say.

**Weak.** The housing talk is the tip of the iceberg.

**Use.** The guest says the city allows too few new homes, and one renter's rent rose by a third.

## Prefer the short word

Never use a long word where a short one will do.

If a short word names the same fact, use it. Keep a long word only when the short one would name a different fact.

**Weak.** The municipality implemented insufficient residential construction in proximity to employment.

**Use.** The city built too few homes near jobs.

## Cut a word that does no work

If it is possible to cut a word out, always cut it out.

Read the sentence without the word. If the fact is unchanged, the word goes. "It is important to note that", "basically", and "in order to" fail that test.

**Weak.** It is important to note that the guest basically said that the city allows too few new homes.

**Use.** The guest says the city allows too few new homes.

## Name who did it

Never use the passive where you can use the active.

Name the person or the body that acted, when the cues name them. "It was argued" hides the guest. "The guest says" does not.

**Weak.** It was argued that too few homes are being built near jobs.

**Use.** The guest says the city builds too few homes near jobs.

Be specific about the name, use first name to refer to a person, and use the last name if in the same context more than one people share the same first name. "The guest says" is ambiguous, "Jack says" is specific.

**Weak.** The guest says it is hard to get people to say yes.
**Use.** Peter says it is hard to get people to say yes.

## Use an everyday word

Never use a foreign phrase, a scientific word or a jargon word if you can think of an everyday English equivalent.

Say the fact in words the reader already uses. Keep the speaker's term when the request is about that term, and put the everyday meaning beside it.

**Weak.** Zoning externalities suppress housing supply elasticity.

**Use.** Rules on what a lot may hold keep new homes scarce.

## Break a rule sooner than write something barbarous

Break any of these rules sooner than say anything outright barbarous.

Barbarous means false, cruel, or so unclear that the reader cannot retell the cue. A short, active sentence that invents an actor has failed.

The cues say the city downzoned three blocks, and the request asks what that did. Keep the word, and say what it means.

**Use.** The city downzoned three blocks, so each lot may hold less floor space.

The cues say the rent rose by a third and do not say who raised it. Do not invent a landlord.

**Use.** Rent rose by a third.
